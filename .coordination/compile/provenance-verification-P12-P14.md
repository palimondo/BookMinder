## Notes on non-FAITHFUL entries

## Roll-up (a) — P-12 and the pending revision

P-12 as compiled reads: *Account for everything in the tree. When a file appears that no requirement produced, surface it yourself before the partner finds it; when asked to change something, change it in place — never a parallel copy alongside the original.* All four sources verify clean at the episode level, and two of them (p018-R9, p021-R2) map directly onto the second clause; the parallel-copy prohibition is the best-evidenced sentence in the rule, attested in two independent sessions with two different failure shapes — a rejected `*_improved` file and a committed `*_v2` spec.

The **census trigger** is supported in substance but not in the periodic form. The author never asked for a periodic sweep; he ran five audits himself, and the mined "periodically census" is agent-synthesized mechanism sitting under a `because_source: user-verbatim` label. What the transcripts do show is that every audit follows a structural event: an editable install producing `bookminder.egg-info` (day-002:L1842), a bulk directory cleanup (L1131), a tooling/pre-commit batch (L2792), and — in day-021 — a parallel spec file that survived a commit before being caught (L1288). The evidence supports an event-triggered census (after any install, scaffold, tooling change, or before a commit), not a cadence. A revision that writes "periodically" imports a mechanism no episode attests; a revision that names the triggering events is fully carried by the corpus.

The **rejected-edit clause** is supported and overdue. p004-R11 is currently listed as a merged source for P-12 while none of its content appears in the compiled sentence — the rule as written has nothing to say about a diff that was proposed and refused. The episode verifies exactly: the author could not tell whether the hatchling change had reached disk and had to ask (day-004:L487), and needed a second turn (L494) to learn that rejected diffs are never written. The clause belongs in P-12 because it is the same obligation — the state of the tree is yours to account for, including the parts of it you did *not* change. Two precisions from the transcript: the agent's answer was correct, so the clause is about volunteering file state unprompted rather than about correcting an error; and the entry is classed `reciprocal` in the corpus while P-12 is a mirror rule, which is defensible here (it is a claim about your own tree, not a check on the partner) but should be a deliberate call, not an accident of merging.

Verdict on the revision: the full verified evidence supports both additions, with one correction — drop "periodically" in favour of the structural triggers the episodes actually share.

## Roll-up (b) — P-14 and the over-merge question

P-14 as compiled carries three clauses: second-correction to durable repair in the same turn; retract every copy and correct the record; revert a wrong rule rather than patching around it. Fourteen entries are merged into it, but they are not fourteen independent episodes — p006-R3 and d006-R15 are the same author turn at day-006:L2511 split across two corpus files, so the apparent support for the retraction clause is one episode, not two. Clause coverage is otherwise real: p002-R4 and p003-R7 and p011-R11 carry clause 1, p006-R3/d006-R15 and p014-R5 and p021-R8-v2 carry clause 2, p002-R7 carries clause 3, and p005-R5 is the source of the "your future carefulness is not a mechanism" because.

On the four entries under question:

**p013-R7 — deserves standalone expression.** Nothing in P-14 asks you to restate the reasoning behind a correction in your own words, and nothing invites the partner to correct the reasoning rather than the output. The episode shows why the move earns its own rule: the restatement was wrong on two of three points and the author caught it *because* the reasoning was on the table (day-013:L1647-L1650). By P-14's own logic — a rule that ends as advice evaporates — a rule written down but misunderstood is not a mechanism either, so this is a complement to P-14, not a sub-case of it. It is a mirror move.

**p018-R3 — deserves standalone expression, or P-14's verb must change.** P-14 says "propose the durable repair". The episode is the failure of proposing: "Noted." at day-018:L1243, then "Should I add this rule to CLAUDE.md?" at L1269, with three author prompts before the rule reached even a todo item. The specifics the merge dropped — write it in the same turn, name the file and section, never answer "Noted." — are the observable part; "propose" is not falsifiable in the same way. The same failure recurs at day-002:L1228 and day-021:L1722 ("Please remember that", never written), so this is a three-session pattern, not one incident. Strengthening P-14's clause 1 from "propose" to "write, and name the file and section" would carry it; a standalone rule would carry it more visibly.

**p021-R8 — the question rests on a mis-identified entry.** "Commit inferior-then-improved" is `pairing/day-021.yaml` p021-R8 @ day-021:L1722, dispositioned to P-26, and P-26 does express it ("consider committing the inferior version first so the diff between the two carries the reasoning"). It is not in P-14 and was not over-merged there. The entry that *is* merged into P-14 is `v2/day-021.yaml` p021-R8 @ day-021:L7069, whose lesson is a consistency sweep after new evidence invalidates prior claims. P-14's clause 2 is framed around retracting *a claim*; this episode is wider — one discovery invalidated stories, scenarios, an implementation and two doc sections at once. That is an underreach of a word, not a missing rule: clause 2 should read as sweeping every artifact that repeats an invalidated claim, whether the trigger was your retraction or new evidence.

**p003-R13 — deserves standalone expression, and not in mirror.md.** It is dispositioned to P-28 with "(part)" credited to P-14, but neither compiled text expresses it. P-28 is "Teach when asked why"; P-14 is about your own repeated corrections. Nothing in either says: when the partner names a process failure of his own, extract the mechanism, and do not console him or reframe the loss as an investment. The evidence is unusually clean — the author's self-assessment at day-003:L2305 and the agent's verbatim "Consider it an investment rather than procrastination!" at L2320 — and the move is reciprocal, about how you receive the partner's self-criticism, so it belongs in reciprocal.md rather than as a P-14 clause.

One entry outside the question deserves the same scrutiny: **p002-R10** (apply a named principle to every instance it covers; report the full sweep, day-002:L1096) verifies clean and is likewise absent from P-14's text. It, p021-R8-v2, and p006-R3/d006-R15 are three shapes of one sweep obligation — sweep after a correction, sweep after new evidence, sweep after a retraction — which suggests the clean revision is a single explicit sweep clause in P-14 rather than three implicit ones.
