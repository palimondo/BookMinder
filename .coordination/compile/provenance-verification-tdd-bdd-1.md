## Roll-ups (rules where I propose a change)

**T-09 — add the "disclosure is not a licence" clause.** As compiled, "Confirm a domain hypothesis against the real data source before writing it into a story card, a filter, or a spec, and state explicitly when your evidence set cannot falsify it" reads as two parallel options, and the second is the cheaper one. The day-011 episode is the case where an agent took the cheaper one in good faith — it named the fixture's blind spot precisely, twice — and was refused. Proposed addition after the second clause: *— and a stated limitation is a report, not a permission to proceed: the author refused a documented-with-caveats write-up plus a backlog item for a better fixture, and required the query against the real library (day-011:L7144-L7172), which then disproved the assumption (L7653).* Nothing else in T-09 or its neighbours carries this; T-76's "explore the real system read-only" is about fixture construction, not about the moment you decide a caveat is enough.

**T-10 — drop or re-source the "re-run after every edit" clause, and restore the real-run qualifier.** Two changes, both grounded in T-10's own sources. First, "re-run it after every edit to the spec itself" enters T-10 only through d017-R14, whose loc holds no author turn and whose quote belongs to the day-017 rename correction (day-017:L1608, already T-22). Neither d002-R1 (day-002:L1516) nor d010-R1 (day-010:L2881) supports it. It should be dropped, or re-sourced from an episode where re-running after a spec edit was actually taught. Second, the clause d010-R1 does support is missing: propose *…before writing any implementation, and say so explicitly — including when the defect was just observed in a real run on a real machine, because real-world feedback is not a licence to bypass RED (day-010:L2872-L2881, reverted at L2928).* With the unsupported clause removed and this one added, every word of T-10 traces to an author turn.

**T-17 — say what the GREEN commit may contain.** Propose appending to T-17: *— and commit only what the red spec drove: read the staged diff and back out any production change no spec demanded before the commit lands (day-016:L7169-L7190, where an unspecified `ZSTATE = 6` edit had to be reverted out of the green commit).* T-98 governs the commit *message* against the diff and T-15 governs how much you implement, but nothing currently tells you to inspect the tree at the commit boundary of the cycle, which is where the day-016 episode was caught.

**T-20 — name the unit of "one refactoring".** T-20's episode does not end at the author's sentence. Two turns later the author rejected the commit the agent had prepared: "nah, we don't need to inline one by one - inine all, and commit" (day-020:L1862, recurring at L24347). One refactoring per commit therefore means the whole of that refactoring applied everywhere it applies — the failure mode is symmetric, and the trickle version is as unreviewable as the mixture. Propose appending: *— and one refactoring means all of it: apply the change to every site it covers in that commit, never one call site per commit (day-020:L1862).* This is the refactor-phase twin of T-22's one-sweep rename and T-24's fix-at-the-origin, and neither of those covers a refactoring split site-by-site.
